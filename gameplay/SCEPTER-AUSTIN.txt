Select Combat subtopic? circ

   circle

     Circling your opponent involves running round and round
your opponent in  a  circle,  thus  causing  him  to  become
disoriented.   It is a defensive move.

     For example:

          Do? CIRCLE WIZARDRESS
          Ok
          You circle her!

     The chance of successfully disorienting your opponent depends
on your dexterity.  

     A player will be disoriented for 8 seconds.  A monster for
two rounds.

Select Combat subtopic? backst

   backstab

     Backstabbing involves sneaking up behind an unsuspecting 
victim to effect maximum damage.  For example:

     Do? HIDE
     You hide in the shadows.
     You encounter a guard.
     Do? BACKSTAB GUARD

     You must be hidden for the backstab to be successful.  
If  you  aren't  hidden or if the victim spies your approach,
the backstab will certainly fail.

     A successful backstab gives +5 to hit and +5 damage.

     An unsuccessful backstab gives -5 to hit plus a free
round for the victim.

     You  will  lose piety points if you kill another player
with a backstab.

Select Combat subtopic? thrust

   thrust

     A  thrust  involves  hurling  or  plunging  your weapon
directly into your opponent to effect maximum damage.  It is
frequently used as the 'coup de grace' of the battle.

     The thrust is a very strenuous move  and  leaves  you  
vulnerable to  a  counter-attack.    It  should be used with
discretion.

     A thrust gives you +min(your_level,4) to hit.

     For example:

         Do? HIT ELDOR

         Do? THRUST
         You killed him!
         ### Hacker, the Fighter, just killed Eldor mercilessly!

     Attacking a player far below your own level is considered
merciless.  Piety points are deducted from your total.

Select Combat subtopic? feint

   feint

     A feint is involves lunging at your opponent and then 
deflecting your blow at the last moment.  It can cause your
opponent to flinch, duck, cover his eyes, etc.  Although
feinting causes no damage it is still considered an offensive
move.

     For example:

     Do? PARRY KNIGHT
     You missed!
     Do? FEINT
     Didn't work!

     A good feint requires a high dexterity.  A player will be
disoriented for 10 seconds.  A monster for three rounds.

Select Lore City subtopic? g

      Geography


                            Port Haven Geography

      [ Compiled from the notes and writings of the Surveyor, R.T.S. ]

The town is situation on an island in the mouth of the River Ember.  To the
east is a forested mountain range which runs roughly north-south.  To the west
is a vast plain that gradually rises toward a much more distant range of
mountains.  Draining of the plain is to the east until the mountains are 
encountered, whence it joins the south-flowing Ember.

The island at the mouth of the river was once a segment of the mountain cliffs
to the west, but an ancient earthquake broke loose a large segment of
erosion-weakened rock which fell and blocked, temporarily, the river mouth.
The lake which formed to the north was once large, but the river has worn
outlets on either side of the island.

The island itself is relatively high in the north -- natural cliffs along the
lake side have been fortified by the city wall.  The southern side of the 
island, particularly the south city wall, is alluvial deposit; low, flat and
More...
sandy.  The natural peninsula to the west and the man-made sea wall to the
east help keep the port from silting up, but during the summer months a nasty
sand bar can develop across the mouth of the bay.  Fortunately the sand bar
is cleared out each winter by storms sweeping in from the southwest.

The southwestern sector of the city wall contains the main city drainage
outlet, which flows past the slums on one side and the swamp on the other.
With the prevailing westerlies, all of the southern area of the island has a
distinctive aroma.

Select Lore City subtopic? gate

      Gates

Three large gates control access to the inner city.  To the south, a large
gate gives access to the southern part of the island.  To the west a gate
opens beside a bridge across the West Ember.  The gate to the east is near
the East Ember Ferry.  Each gate has its own guardhouse; the South Gate's
guardhouse is larger as it contains the City Guard barracks.

Each gate has several names.  Officially they are known as the South, East
and West gates.  However, being decorated with bas-relief sculpture to reflect
their primary traffic, the townsfolk will often refer to them by the common
names of Hero, Horse and Garden Gates.  At other times the gate names refer
to the areas reached: Sea, Mountain and Plains Gates.

The dwellers in the south of the island, outside the walls, occasionally make
references to the Main Gate.  In addition, those high enough in society to 
live within the city walls refer to the gates by what they bring in:  Fish,
Traders and Farmers gates.

Finally, although never brought up in polite company, there's the small gate
in the city wall that is the outlet for sewage.  Officially it is named the
Water Gate, but is vulgarly referred to as the Anus.  Because of its 
More...
association with things best not discussed, occasionally, a scandal
surrounding an official's hidden corruption is referred to as his "water-gate.

      Economy


                            Port Haven Economy  

    [ Compiled from the notes and writings of the Surveyor, R.T.S. ]

The city is a meeting place for three major economic elements.  One is the
farmland of the plains; grain, cattle, fruit and vegetables arrive both 
overland and by river barge, and are brought into the city by the West Gate.
Caravans coming through the mountain pass bring luxury goods and exotic
spices from the overland trade routes.  Ships eager to pick up cargo gather
at the port, bringing gold and slaves from distant lands in exchange.

The city's most important source of revenue are the markets, primarily the
Farmers' and Traders' markets within the walls, to a lesser extent the Fish
market near the Sea Wall, and unofficially - but still lucratively - the
Slave market.  Taxes are gathered at the gate; market space is rented from 
the city.  The East Ember Ferry is free for those on foot, but caravans must
pay.  Ships not only pay for dock space but for anchorage within the bay.  
Traders with particularly valuable consignments of goods in caravans or on
ships are required to "hire" additional squads of city guards at inflated
More...
rates.

The Cleric Sanctuary is a focus of farm-folks' pilgrimages, so it is near thei
market.  The Mages' Guild often provides special guardians for caravans and is
closer to the Trader's Gate.  The Fighters' Guild supplies manpower for the
City Guard and the trader caravans.

It is possible, though not common, for a slave sold off a trade ship to win hi
freedom in the Arena and join the Fighters' Guild.  The Thieves' Guild provide
an alternate route to the inner city; from their headquarters near the slums
they are rumored to have secret underground passages throughout the island's
rocky foundation.

The Keep is the most heavily protected complex on the island.  It is the 
residence of the nobility, as well as the center of the local government.
Common knowledge has it that the city treasury is filled with fabulous wealth
extracted from the endless trade.  It is also said that the treasure is guarde
by a Demon from hell, but this rumor is very difficult to confirm.

Select Lore Monsters ALL subtopic? cli

         CliffRoc

  The Cliff Roc is a large white and gray bird that populates the snowy
mountains north of the trader's route.  The creature stands about 5' at the
shoulder and has two distinct coats of feathers.  The undercoat is composed
of soft, white downy feathers, and serves to keep the bird warm in cold climes
The outer coat is composed of long white and gray feathers.  The feathers of
Cliff Roc grow very slowly; thus, the bird almost never sheds its feathers.

  The Cliff Rocs, so named for their tendency to nest along the outcroppings
of cliffs and crags, are reputed to be the direct descendants of the avian
mounts which once carried the ice sorcerers to the stronghold in the mountains
During the sorcerer's rather abrupt stay at the stronghold, a few of the great
birds escaped and found themselves in the wild misty mountains, where they had
to adapt to an entirely different lifestyle.

  The sorcerers had valued their mounts not only for their capability of
carrying their riders over great distances through the air, but also for their
fighting skills.  The birds were taught as fledglings to maim and kill upon
command, but such training made them quite dangerous, and even the sorcerers
took precautions.  The birds were kept under harness and saddle at all times,
and were tethered to the ground when not in use.  However, it is unlikely that
More...
such mundane means could have kept captive creatures as strong as the rocs;
thus, many have ventured that the sorcerers kept the creatures in control by
means of magic, else the rocs would have slain their masters long ago.

  In order to make the rocs more formidable in combat, the sorcerers had
adorned them with knifelike attachments to their talons.  These claw spikes
were edged, and enabled the birds to cut and slice at opponents rather than
simply claw at them.  Though the escaped rocs had no need for harness, tether
or saddle once they were free of the sorcerers, the claw spikes were kept for
their usefulness in combat.  The creatures had, after all, learned a great
deal in their captivity, and part of what they learned was that a strong
offense was necessary for survival in their world, and that in the end it
might be the only thing that would prevent them from future enslavement.
Therefore, no Cliff Roc will ever intentionally leave a human unmolested, nor
are the birds likely to flee from combat with any humanoid creature.  It is 
said that the creatures are not actually evil or malicious; however, they are
at least extremely aggressive, and since they are good fighters as well, they
are exceedingly dangerous.

  Cliff Rocs prey mostly on small mountain goats, though they can handle much
More...
larger game if necessary.  They can carry a tiger-sized creature through the
air, albeit with some difficulty.  They generally hunt alone rather than in
groups, although a mother will often take her fledglings along with her on the
hunt in order to teach them the technique.  A Cliff Roc hunts by sighting its
quarry from the air and then making a single dive, grasping its target tightly
with its claws at the bottom of the dive, and then winging rapidly upward.

  Although they generally fight and hunt alone rather than in groups, the Rocs
do have a social order.  Rank is determined by a combination of age and
fighting skill; the weaker fighters are not likely to live long anyway,     
especially considering their aggressive nature.  Fledglings are low on the
pecking order, as they are generally not very skilled at hunting and fighting.
The birds do not actually designate leaders, but the older, more skilled Rocs
have some semblance of authority among their clan.

  Most Cliff Rocs nest in large caves along the sides of cliffs and mountains.
They prefer locations which can only be reached by flying.  A good-sized cave
will house four to five Cliff Roc families, which consist of the mother and
father and two to three generations of fledglings.  The young are expelled fro
the family once they reach maturity, in 3 to 4 years.
More...

  Female Rocs lay eggs once a year.  Mature females generally lay two eggs
(sometimes three, when the hunting has been good), while younger ones lay only
one.  The eggs do not need much warmth and do not require constant attention
from the female, which leaves both the mother and the father free to hunt.
However, many Roc families will require that the eggs be guarded at all times,
and this duty is often rotated among the older fledglings.

  Fledgling Rocs are born with a single coat of gray down.  The outer coat,
which at first consists entirely of gray feathers, will have grown in
completely by the time the fledgling is 6 months old.  Subsequent years will
see the shedding of the gray undercoat to be replaced by white down and the
addition of white feathers to the outer coat.  Fledglings are first taught
to fly when their first outer coat has grown in.  (Attempts to teach them
to fly earlier than that will prove fruitless and possibly dangerous, as the
long feathers of the outer coat on the wings are necessary for keeping the
bird airborne.)

Subject: "Sokoban scorefile changes"
The score keeping process of Sokoban has been modified somewhat with the
primary goal of allowing the best scores for all levels to be recorded,
not just for the highest levels. The total number of scorefile entries has
been increased from 50 to 250 and each player can only have 1 entry per
level. If you beat your previous score for a level, your score will be
updated in the scorefile. Any previously played level can be replayed
as many times as you want to try and make the best score for that level.
When a score is added to the scorefile, you will be told the overall rank
and the rank for that level. You will then have the option of viewing the
scorefile. You can select to see the entire scorefile, or the scores for
any single level. The default is to display the scores for the level you
have just completed.

Subject: "Policy on Player vs Player."
              Player vs. Player combat in Scepter - System Policy
  Every few months the subject comes up -  what about players attacking and 
 terrorizing other players?  In the past we've tried to simply let users know
 that although it is technically possible within the limits of the software,
 it is not the point of the game, nor is it approved of.  Still, some people
 seem to need it spelled out more clearly for them - therefore this document,
 which is now a part of the system policy.
  The answer is simple - that is not what we want the system to be used for,
 and it is not supported.  Users that do this will be asked to stop, and if
 they do not cease then they risk losing all further system access.
  We recognize that in a battle game it will rarely happen that the actions of
 one member of a group could have negative, perhaps fatal, results for one or
 more other members of the group.  Note the use of the word 'rare' above.  We
 are not attempting to set up rules to punish or reprimand those that get into
 this kind of a situation by accident.  We are, however, fully aware that
 these kinds of situations don't happen often and are easy to identify in
 comparison to deliberate and premeditated actions.
  It's been suggested that the above is 'just another opinion'.  That is not
 correct.  It is the official opinion of the owners of this system, and use of
 this system implies acceptance of this policy.
  Many long and hard hours go into enhancing this system.  We don't intend to
 waste those hours building in limits in software, when there are many better
 and more beneficial uses for that time.
                               The Sysops
